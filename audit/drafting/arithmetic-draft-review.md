## Checked load-bearing proof chain

1. **Definitions and finite identities (`definitions.tex:5–29,33–62,77–136`).** Strict roughness and inclusive smoothness agree throughout, including the integer one. The parent chain adds distinct increasing primes, so the finite geometric-series inverse has entry `mu(i/j)` exactly on ancestor pairs. Its first column is the Möbius vector, and the determinant update is `1 + sum_{j=2}^n mu(j) = M(n)`. The polynomial-in-update-parameter argument proves the adjugate formula even at a zero determinant. The column sums give the stated coordinates of `w_n`; squaring gives exactly `E + (M-1)^2 + n-Q`, with no square root confusion in `Q`. The example at `n=6` is consistent. Disjoint parent-column supports and the counts of matrix entries give both final norm identities.

2. **Classical input ranges (`arithmetic.tex:19–107`).** The second Mertens exponent follows because `3/5 - 7/12 = 1/60`. In the smooth range, `log z=L(1-o(1))`, `t` is comparable to `sqrt(L log L)`, and `v` is comparable to `sqrt(L/log L)`, uniformly. These substitutions meet both displayed growth conditions and both extra `k=1` boundary conditions with room to spare. The expansion is genuinely two-term, and its additive error has the required relative size `O((log(v+1)/t)^2)`. Only arguments tending uniformly to infinity are used in the derivative estimates. This review does not substitute the insufficient first-order smooth estimate for the stated two-term theorem.

3. **Exact convolutions and upper bound (`arithmetic.tex:111–165`).** The local Euler factors cancel precisely the primes at most `y`. Both finite convolutions and their coefficient at one follow. The positive majorant really is monotone in `y`, though `R` need not be. With `h=L^(9/10)`, the Mertens exponent is `h^(7/12)=L^(21/40)`; its ratio to `s` diverges. The all-smooth reciprocal sum is bounded by its Euler product. Rankin's factor has the correct sign for the tail `a>x exp(-h)`. Higher prime powers are uniformly bounded once `alpha<=1/4`; the displayed integral estimate controls the prime terms. The resulting product logarithm is `O(log L+u)=o(s)`, `alpha h=o(s)`, and `u log u=(1/(2c)+o(1))s`. Thus the positive-majorant version of the lemma is established as well as the signed-sum bound.

4. **Cancellation-preserving truncation (`arithmetic.tex:179–235`).** For integer `K`, the exact finite tail is the sum over smooth `a<=x/K` of `M(x/a)-M(K)`. The equality endpoint contributes zero. Every dyadic `q` used before `H` satisfies the smooth-input window; cutting the last interval avoids exceeding it. Integrating the logarithmic Dickman derivative gives the shift bound with the stated uniform constant. The absorption condition is explicitly `beta v^(1-7/12) = O(L^(-1/8) sqrt(log L))=o(1)` at the upper endpoint. Counting `O(L)` dyadic blocks costs only `O(log L)` in the exponent, less than `(log K)^(7/12) ~ (log L)^(7/6)`. Above `H`, `L^(21/40)` dominates both `|log rho(u)|=O(s)` and `|log beta|`. All three parts are therefore `o(x rho(u) beta)` uniformly. Taking absolute values after swapping the tail does not destroy the necessary Möbius cancellation.

5. **Short expansion and surviving sign (`arithmetic.tex:237–279`).** The derivative bounds and `beta log K=o(1)` give the two displayed Taylor errors. Absolute summation contributes at most the cubic logarithmic factor. Mertens decay makes both Möbius moment tails convergent, including the logarithmic weight, and justifies the limit and derivative at `sigma=1`. The simple zeta pole gives `A_0(infinity)=0` and `A_1(infinity)=-1`, so the surviving main term is **negative** `x rho'(u)/t`. The error-to-main-term ratio is `O(beta(1+log K)^3)=o(1)`; the moment errors decay faster still. This proves the relative, uniformly nonzero bridge, not merely its logarithmic scale.

6. **All fixed moments, maximum, and concentration (`arithmetic.tex:283–366`).** The bin bound sums the integer reciprocal-power tail, yielding `(q-1)a+q/(2b)`, not an erroneous prime-count factor. Small primes are handled through the positive-majorant lemma at the enlarged cutoff; their `x=n/p` still satisfies `log x=L+O(s)`. Large primes have cost `(q-1)C_0`. Taking fixed endpoints and a finite mesh before the asymptotic limit gives the stated fixed-`q` upper bound. The minimum is `sqrt(2q(q-1))` at `sqrt(q/(2(q-1)))`. On each dyadic prime block, the relative bridge and PNT give the matching lower exponent uniformly. Pointwise minimization yields the separate maximum constant `sqrt(2)` at `c=1/sqrt(2)`. For `q=2`, the unique minimum of `c+1/c` is two at one; compact and remote ranges retain a strictly positive gap off any fixed central band. No unjustified varying-`q` passage is used.

7. **Composite energy and norm ratios (`arithmetic.tex:393–477`).** The unique factorization `j=ap` imposes `p>P^+(a)` and includes `a=1`. The `a>exp(3s)` tail is `O(n^2 exp(-3s))=o(E_P)`. The floor identity is exact because `pd` is integral. The minimum ambient integer `N_a` tends to infinity and `s(N_a)/s(n)->1` uniformly for `a<=exp(3s)`, allowing a single eventual concentration theorem to control all these integers. The complement of the displayed central band is outside the `delta=1/3` band at every such `N_a` eventually. In the central region both bridge applications satisfy `log x=L+O(s)`, while the Dickman arguments differ by at most six. Dividing the second-derivative bound by the first-derivative lower bound gives the stated logarithmic derivative bound for `-rho'`. Integration yields `a^(-2+epsilon)` domination with any fixed `epsilon<1`; the dominating sequence is summable. For each fixed `a` the ratio tends uniformly to `a^-2`, the prime restriction disappears, and eventually `ap<=n` throughout the central band. Dominated convergence therefore gives `sum mu(a)^2/a^2=zeta(2)/zeta(4)=15/pi^2`. Finally both remaining terms of the exact norm identity are negligible on the prime-energy scale. This proves the multiplicative ratios without assuming a multiplicative equivalent for `E_P`.

8. **Support extremizers, including the transition (`arithmetic.tex:481–539`).** Least-prime grouping has the correct negative sign. Hölder gives saving `sqrt(2 theta)-kappa theta`. For `kappa>1/sqrt(2)`, its maximizing fixed parameter is `theta=1/(2 kappa^2)`; otherwise the independent maximum bound gives the endpoint value. For strict `kappa<1/sqrt(2)`, the extremal block contains more than the required `floor(exp(kappa s))` primes eventually. For `kappa>=1/sqrt(2)`, taking the entire block at `c=kappa` has size asymptotic to `exp(kappa s)/(kappa s)`, hence meets the ceiling even at equality. Its total exponent is `1/(2 kappa)`. The two branch values agree at the transition. Eventual negativity means indicator weights suffice for these constructions; no sharpness assertion for an arbitrary prescribed support is smuggled in.

9. **Exceptional coefficient counts (`arithmetic.tex:541–570`).** The Markov minimizer and value are correct: `q=(1+lambda/sqrt(lambda^2-2))/2` and `(lambda+sqrt(lambda^2-2))/2`. The reverse bound uses a fixed block strictly inside the threshold interval, so its `o(1)` cannot violate the cutoff. The case below `sqrt(2)` follows from the maximum. At equality, taking an infimum of fixed-`q` limsup bounds gives the stated upper bound without claiming a lower bound or uniform moment estimate.

10. **Thin-support harmonic series (`arithmetic.tex:574–642`).** The fixed weights satisfy the finite support theorem at each sufficiently large integer; the floor ceiling is automatic from integer cardinality. The change of variables in the tail integral has the stated Jacobian and loses only a polynomial factor. This proves convergence in natural order and the exponential tail saving. Independently identifying the sum is necessary and is done: the least-prime Euler product is exact for `sigma>1`; thin support implies `sum_{p in S} log(2p)/p<infinity`; the product bound dominates the prime sum uniformly as `sigma` decreases to one; and `1/zeta(sigma)` tends to zero. The established integrability of `|T(t)|/t^2` justifies the final Abel limit. The smaller-support variant follows directly from the maximum and the same convergence argument. Absolute convergence of the original harmonic series is neither used nor claimed.
