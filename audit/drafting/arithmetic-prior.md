## What could be distinct

Write s = sqrt(log n loglog n) and b_p = R(n/p,p). The candidate contribution is the distribution of these coefficients: fixed q>1 moments with exponent sqrt(2q(q-1)), the support-constrained extremum with exponent g(kappa), and the precise prime-to-full energy transfer. The coefficients and weighted least-prime problem themselves are historical. A new title for that problem, or its matrix realization, does not establish a new arithmetic theorem.

The elementary least-prime grouping identity is exact: sum_{2<=m<=n} mu(m)a_{P^-(m)} = -sum_p a_p b_p. It is not interchangeable with an unrestricted divisor duality or a smooth-Möbius theorem: the latter restricts the greatest prime factor and has different coefficients. The prior local audit attributes the unrestricted bounded-weight supremum and its l1 identity to Alladi 1982, p.86, equations (1.1)–(1.3). This turn could verify the bibliographic record but could not freshly inspect that page or the theorem pages. Preserve that provenance distinction.

An l1 theorem alone does not determine a q>1 norm or decreasing rearrangement. Consequently the sparse-support law is not *logically* subsumed merely by Alladi's unrestricted supremum. Conversely, any historical uniform relative estimate for the same b_p throughout log p comparable to s could make almost all of the proposed moment and support constants routine corollaries. That exact uniformity is the unresolved comparison, not a search for similar titles.

## Verified smooth source and range

Nathan McNew, *The most frequent values of the largest prime divisor function*, **Experimental Mathematics 26 (2017), 210–224**; publication metadata verified on the [author's research list](https://www.nathanmcnew.com/math.html). [Accessible author manuscript](https://www.nathanmcnew.com/PopularPrimes.pdf), printed pp.3–5: equations (7), (13)–(14), (17); Theorems 1.1–1.2 on p.2. Equation (13) is the derivative expansion of Lambda, with fixed expansion order, a stated boundary condition, and coefficients from (s-1)zeta(s)/s. Equation (14) specializes to two terms with relative error O((log(u+1)/log y)^2). Its coefficient is gamma-1. Equation (17) gives the Dickman ratio needed for the first derivative. These are explicit statements, not inferred from an abstract. The source's largest-prime frequency is Psi(n/p,p), not b_p. Theorems 1.1–1.2 give its sharper maximum location and value. No literal rough-moment theorem was identified there. Original Saias and Tenenbaum proofs were not read in this turn.

Application check, independently calculated: put L=log n, t=log p comparable to sqrt(L log L), and z=(n/p)/k with log k<=L^(9/10). Then log z=L-o(L), v=log z/t tends uniformly to infinity, and t dominates every fixed power of loglog z. Thus both y>(log z)^(1+epsilon) and y>exp((loglog z)^(5/3+epsilon)) hold with margin. For the fixed order k=1 in the source expansion (distinct from the convolution index), each boundary ratio involving v and j tends to infinity, whereas loglog y/log y tends to zero. There is no approach to an integer-v boundary in the small-u regime. The exponentially small Psi/Lambda error is negligible relative to the displayed squared logarithmic error. This discharges the stated-range concern for the S input in lower-bound-development.md, conditional on the source theorem as stated.

The local Möbius inversion uses cancellation of the first density term and retains its derivative. That is a real connecting argument; a one-term relative approximation would not justify it. But it uses classical inputs and has not here been shown absent from the classical rough-sum literature. Alladi's presently unverified additive error quotation cannot replace it: at the saddle, the derivative main term is exponentially smaller than a typical power-log additive error.
